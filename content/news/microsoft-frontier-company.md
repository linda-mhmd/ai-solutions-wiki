The big cloud providers are deciding that selling AI tools is not enough; customers also need people who can build with them. On 2 July 2026 Microsoft announced Microsoft Frontier Company, a new operating business that puts engineers inside customer teams to deliver AI systems, backed by a large investment. It is a direct sign that "who builds it" is becoming as much a product as the models themselves.

## What happened

Announced by Judson Althoff, CEO of Microsoft Commercial Business, **Microsoft Frontier Company** is a new operating business with:

- A **USD 2.5 billion investment** (about €2.3 billion).
- **6,000 industry and engineering experts** embedded directly with customers to build and operate AI systems.
- **Rodrigo Kede Lima** appointed as President.

The model is embedded delivery: rather than hand a customer a platform and documentation, Microsoft places its own engineers inside the customer's teams to design, build, and run the AI work.

## Why it matters for builders

This is the hyperscalers concluding that the bottleneck to AI adoption is not the models but the shortage of people who can turn them into working, governed systems. AWS made a similar move with its [forward-deployed engineers](/news/aws-forward-deployed-engineers/); Microsoft is now industrialising the same idea at scale.

For the services market, this reshapes the field that consultancies, agencies, and independent engineers operate in. When a hyperscaler will embed 6,000 engineers with customers, the differentiator for smaller providers shifts to what the giants do not do well: deep domain knowledge, independence from a single vendor's stack, honest architecture advice, and the flexibility to work across clouds. The demand signal is unambiguous, though: the scarce resource in enterprise AI is people who can ship it, which is good news for anyone building that skill.
