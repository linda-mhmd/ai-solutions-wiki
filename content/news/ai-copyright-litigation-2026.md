The legal front around AI widened in 2026, moving beyond the original training-data copyright suits into discovery fights, answer-engine cases, and the first rulings on whether model weights are infringing copies. Nothing here is settled law yet, but the direction matters for anyone training on or building with third-party content. Note that court statuses change quickly; the items below reflect the record as researched and each should be checked against the live docket before relying on it.

## What happened

The **New York Times** and other news plaintiffs filed a motion on 9 July 2026 asking the court to sanction OpenAI, alleging it deleted ChatGPT logs in violation of a preservation order (case 1:23-cv-11195, S.D.N.Y.). It is a filed motion, not a ruling, but it is the first major spoliation fight in the flagship AI-copyright case.

In **Bartz v. Anthropic** (N.D. Cal.), the roughly $1.5 billion settlement, the largest US copyright settlement on record, had its final approval hearing on 14 May 2026. **Final approval was granted on 20 July 2026** (see the October update below; an earlier version of this entry said final approval had not been entered, which was already wrong when it was last verified). Meanwhile the litigation broadened: Encyclopaedia Britannica and Merriam-Webster sued OpenAI (13 March), BMG sued Anthropic (17 March), and CNN sued Perplexity (28 May), pushing the fight into reference works, music, and answer engines. In the UK, the **Getty v. Stability AI** judgment (November 2025) held that model weights are not "infringing copies" while finding limited trademark infringement, with an appeal window in early 2026. On the regulatory side, Ireland's Data Protection Commission opened a GDPR inquiry into X and xAI on 17 February 2026 over non-consensual deepfakes generated by Grok.

## Why it matters for builders

Two practical signals. First, **provenance and retention are now legal exposure.** The OpenAI sanctions fight is about deleted logs; if you build a product on top of an LLM, how you log, retain, and can produce records is becoming a discovery question. Second, **the "is training fair use" question is still open,** and it is now being tested across text, music, reference works, and answer engines at once, so a ruling in any one case could ripple.

The Getty UK holding, that weights are not copies, is an early data point favouring model builders on one narrow question, but it is under appeal and is UK law only. The safe posture is unchanged: know the provenance of your training and retrieval data, keep records, and treat "fair use will protect us" as an unsettled bet. ## Update, September 2026

**Doe v. GitHub (Ninth Circuit, 16 September 2026).** A unanimous panel upheld the dismissal of the open-source programmers' DMCA §1202(b) claims against GitHub, Microsoft and OpenAI. It held that Copilot and Codex, as the complaint describes them, generate new code that never contained copyright management information, so no CMI was "removed or altered." The court rejected the district court's strict "identicality" rule, found the plaintiffs had standing, and expressly left open ordinary copyright-infringement claims. It did not decide a training-stage CMI theory, because that theory had not been preserved. Breach-of-contract claims remain pending in the trial court. Full write-up: [Ninth Circuit upholds dismissal of DMCA claims in Doe v. GitHub](/news/doe-v-github-ninth-circuit/).

**China proposes a fair-use regime for AI training.** China's National Copyright Administration published its copyright development plan for 2026-2030, released online on 7 September 2026 and reported by Digital Watch on 9 September. Among other measures, it proposes developing a **fair-use system for AI training data**, which it describes as balancing innovation with protection of creators' rights. The plan sets a direction rather than specific rules. If adopted, it would settle in policy a question that US courts are still deciding case by case.

## Update, October 2026: the Anthropic settlement is approved, and payments are scheduled

**Bartz v. Anthropic is past final approval.** On 20 July 2026 Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín signed the "Order Granting Final Approval of Class Action Settlement; Granting in Part Motion for Attorney's Fees, Reimbursement of Expenses, and Plaintiff Service Awards; Judgment" (Dkt. 680; the caption gives Case No. 3:24-cv-05417-AMO, while the court's page headers read 4:24-cv-05417-AMO). The court considered "submissions from fifty-four class members and putative class members" and states: "Final judgment is hereby ENTERED". What it decided:

- **Fees cut by almost half.** Class counsel asked for 12.5 percent, $187,500,000 (already reduced from 20 percent in their opening brief). The court awarded **$101,561,111**, "nearly 6.8% percent of the non-reversionary Settlement Fund", using a lodestar multiplier of 3.75, and holds back 10 percent of it until a post-distribution accounting. Arithmetic: $101,561,111 is 6.77 percent of $1.5 billion.
- **Expenses and service awards.** $2,635,197.46 in expenses plus an $18,220,000 cost reserve, and $15,000 to each of the three class representatives instead of the $50,000 requested.
- **Claims.** The order records a claims rate of "at least 91.3 percent of Works", and an amount per work of "about $3,000, less costs and fees".

**Appeals do not hold up payment.** Class counsel's status report of 2 September 2026 (Dkt. 688) says "No Class Member or Objector filed a timely appeal." Two notices of appeal were filed by counsel excluded from the fee award (one involving Edelson P.C. and Oppenheim + Zebrak, the other Cowan DeBaets Abrahams & Sheppard LLP), and an objector mailed a request to appeal postmarked 25 August. Because the settlement agreement's Effective Date ignores appeals "solely with respect to the Fee Award and/or service awards", the parties told the court "the Effective Date of the Settlement has passed." The same report puts each work's allotment at "approximately $2,203.56 of funds currently in the Settlement Fund", before Anthropic's final payment of $450 million plus interest. The court-approved settlement website, updated under a 15 September order (Dkt. 692), now says the first round of payments is expected "on or before November 15, 2026", and repeats that the Effective Date passed "assuming that no late-filed appeal is permitted to go forward."

What this entry could not verify: the CourtListener docket refused automated access on 25 October 2026, so the Ninth Circuit case numbers of the two fee appeals and the docket numbers of the notices are not given here. The same block applies to **In re OpenAI Copyright Infringement Litigation** (S.D.N.Y. 1:25-md-03143, Judge Sidney H. Stein); this entry does not state that case's status until its docket can be read.
