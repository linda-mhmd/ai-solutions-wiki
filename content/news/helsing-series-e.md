European AI funding has usually meant consumer apps and enterprise software. On 13 July 2026, a different kind of round set a record: Munich-based defence AI company Helsing raised USD 1.8 billion (about €1.7 billion), the largest European defence-technology funding round to date. It is a marker of how much capital is now flowing into applied AI outside the familiar software categories.

## What happened

Per Helsing's newsroom announcement, the company raised a **USD 1.8 billion Series E** (about €1.7 billion) at a valuation of **USD 18 billion** (about €16.6 billion), described as the largest European defence-tech round to date. Helsing builds AI software and systems for defence, and it is one of the most prominent of a new wave of European defence-technology companies.

Helsing's site blocks automated access, so this article stays to the figures the company itself published: the round size, the valuation, and the "largest European defence-tech round" framing. Treat any further operational detail as something to confirm directly with the company.

## Why it matters for builders

Two signals matter here, independent of any view on defence itself. First, the scale: an €18 billion valuation puts a European applied-AI company in a bracket that used to be reserved for US labs and consumer platforms, which reshapes where European AI talent and capital concentrate. Second, the category: serious money is moving into AI applied to specific, regulated, high-stakes domains, not just general-purpose models and chat products.

For anyone building applied AI in Europe, that is context worth tracking. It affects the talent market, the availability of capital for domain-specific AI, and the policy conversation, since defence AI draws regulatory and ethical scrutiny that general software does not.
