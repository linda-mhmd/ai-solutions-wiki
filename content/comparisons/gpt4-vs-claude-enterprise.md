The "GPT-4 versus Claude" framing this page originally carried is long gone. As of September 2026, OpenAI's enterprise lineup runs on the GPT-5.6 family (Sol, Terra, Luna) with GPT-6 Astra just beginning a limited rollout, and Anthropic's runs on Haiku 4.5, Sonnet 5, Opus 5, and Fable 5.1, with a gated Mythos 5.1 above them. Both portfolios are strong, well-supported, and roughly matched on the dimensions a benchmark table measures. That is exactly why a benchmark table is the wrong place to start: when two options are this close on capability, the decision is almost never actually decided by capability. It is decided by a constraint the reader already has — a jurisdictional rule, a contract that names a cloud, an outage the organization cannot absorb, spend already committed elsewhere.

This page follows the wiki's [constraint-driven comparison methodology](/guides/constraint-driven-comparisons/): name the constraint categories that could plausibly rule an option out or shape the tradeoff, mark which ones are hard gates versus which are weighable, and only then look at features — because for most readers, the two model lineups are not competing on a spec sheet so much as being filtered by facts about the reader's own organization that have nothing to do with either vendor's benchmark scores.

## Regulatory and jurisdictional exposure

**The gate here is almost never "GPT versus Claude."** Both OpenAI and Anthropic are US-headquartered companies. Both are therefore providers "subject to US jurisdiction" under the [US CLOUD Act](/glossary/cloud-act/) — the statute compels disclosure based on a provider's possession, custody, or control of data, not on which region the data centre sits in. If an organization's actual constraint is "no US legal-process reach over our data, full stop," that constraint eliminates GPT *and* Claude together, not one relative to the other. Building on Claude instead of GPT does not exit CLOUD Act reach; neither does routing inference through an EU data centre. A reader with that specific constraint needs an EU-incorporated model provider outside this comparison entirely (see [sovereign AI](/glossary/sovereign-ai/) and [data sovereignty](/glossary/data-sovereignty/)) — naming that plainly is more useful than a paragraph implying one of these two options solves it.

What jurisdiction *does* differentiate is cost, and both vendors turn out to publish an explicit number for it, of almost identical size — a genuine parallel worth having precisely, rather than an asymmetry. Anthropic's own pricing page states that pinning inference to `inference_geo: "us"` (a guarantee that processing stays within the United States) carries a flat **1.1x multiplier on every token category** — input, output, cache writes, and cache reads — on the first-party API, on Claude Platform on AWS, and on Claude in Microsoft Foundry's US Data Zone Standard deployment type, with Bedrock and Google Cloud instead following their own independent regional pricing [1]. OpenAI's own developer platform publishes a directly comparable figure for its equivalent product: regional processing (data residency) endpoints — which include a United States region alongside the EU/EEA, UK, and several others — carry a **10% uplift** on standard, batch, and Flex pricing alike, for models released on or after 5 March 2026 [6][8]. That is the same order of magnitude as Anthropic's 1.1x, quoted on OpenAI's own first-party API. What this page could not find is an equivalently explicit, single published multiplier specifically for *Azure* OpenAI or Azure Government's own regional and FedRAMP High options; a reader whose deployment runs through Azure specifically, rather than OpenAI's first-party API, should still get a direct, current quote rather than assume either vendor's first-party figure carries over.

**A separate, genuine gate sits inside each portfolio's own most capable tier.** Anthropic's Mythos 5.1, released 1 September 2026 alongside Fable 5.1, is not self-serve: access runs through the Cyber Verification Program or Life Sciences Verification Program, is currently limited to a small set of vetted US organizations, and Anthropic describes broader access as still being coordinated with the US government [2]. On the same day, OpenAI disclosed that GPT-6 Astra is the first OpenAI model to cross the **"Critical" cybersecurity capability threshold** in its own Preparedness Framework — during evaluation it chained two previously unknown vulnerabilities into a working exploit without a human directing each step — and gated the model accordingly rather than shipping it broadly: initial access on 1 September went to a small set of vetted testers reported to include US government and critical-infrastructure organizations, general rollout followed on 3 September through Daybreak-gated access, and only from there did availability begin expanding to ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise plans and to the API, AWS Bedrock, and Microsoft Azure [3][4][11]. See [Astra becomes the first OpenAI model to cross the 'Critical' cyber threshold](/news/openai-astra-critical-cyber-threshold/) for the full account, including the training-run pause that preceded release. Read together, these are not one vendor being cautious and the other being open — both labs are now gating their most cyber-capable frontier tier behind verification rather than a signup form. For most enterprise buyers this is not the tier they are evaluating (Sonnet, Opus, Haiku, and the Sol/Terra/Luna tiers remain ordinary self-serve API products), but a reader whose actual roadmap depends on frontier-tier cyber or bio capability should budget verification lead time on either side, not assume a plan calling for Mythos- or Astra-class access ships on a normal procurement timeline.

**General-purpose AI obligations under the EU AI Act bind both providers equally.** Both OpenAI and Anthropic are providers of general-purpose AI models placing them on the EU market, and the Article 51–56 GPAI obligations have been enforceable by the AI Office since 2 August 2026 [see [EU AI Act enforcement begins](/news/eu-ai-act-enforcement-begins-2026/)]. Whether a system built on top of either model carries Annex III high-risk obligations depends entirely on what the reader's own downstream system does — employment screening, credit decisions, biometric identification — not on which model API it calls; see [governance thresholds as you scale](/guides/governance-thresholds-as-you-scale/) for exactly where that line sits. Annex III obligations for standalone high-risk systems are, as of the Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744), deferred to 2 December 2027, while Article 50 transparency duties (labelling AI-generated content, disclosing a user is talking to a machine) apply now, regardless of model vendor [see [EU AI Act: what took effect 2 August 2026](/news/eu-ai-act-august-2026-obligations/)]. None of this is a GPT-versus-Claude gate; it is a gate on what the reader is building, applied identically on top of either.

**US domestic policy is genuinely unsettled, and that uncertainty is vendor-neutral too.** Executive Order 14409 and the proposed National AI Legislative Framework push toward one federal standard preempting state AI law, while states keep legislating their own rules regardless: Colorado repealed and rewrote its first-in-nation AI Act via SB26-189, resetting the obligation's effective date to 1 January 2027 rather than cancelling it [see [the US moves to preempt state AI laws](/news/us-ai-policy-preemption-2026/)]. Whichever model a US deployment calls, the open question of which layer of law actually binds it is the same question, decided by litigation and Congress, not by model choice.

## Contractual and customer-driven requirements

This is the category most likely to actually be a hard gate for a specific reader, and it is worth naming precisely because it is not governed by the same logic as a regulation. **If a specific enterprise customer's procurement contract, security questionnaire, or an organization's own existing enterprise agreement names one cloud or one vendor explicitly, that is dispositive — no amount of capability comparison changes it.** An organization contractually restricted to an existing Azure Enterprise Agreement with no AWS or GCP authorized is not weighing GPT against Claude; it has already been told which vendor's models it may call.

The one nuance worth flagging: an "Azure-only" mandate is *not* automatically a gate against Claude specifically, because Anthropic's models are also available through Microsoft Foundry, billed as a Claude Consumption Unit line item on the Azure Marketplace [1][5]. A reader whose constraint looks like "we're an Azure shop" on its face may still have Claude available to them without leaving that contract — worth checking explicitly before assuming the mandate rules it out, rather than either assuming it does or assuming it doesn't.

SOC 2 attestation, a named HIPAA Business Associate Agreement, and similar controls belong in this category too, not folded into "compliance" as if a regulation required them. Both providers offer these on request for enterprise API customers [6][7]; the actual trigger for needing one is a specific customer's procurement process asking for it, not a legal threshold — see [governance thresholds as you scale](/guides/governance-thresholds-as-you-scale/) for why that distinction matters for planning lead time.

## Cost structure and sunk investment

**Existing committed cloud spend changes the marginal economics of this decision independent of headline token price**, and as of September 2026 that consideration has gotten less clean-cut on both sides. Claude is reachable through AWS Bedrock, Google Cloud Vertex AI, Microsoft Foundry, Anthropic's own API, and now Claude Platform on AWS (billed through AWS Marketplace in Claude Consumption Units against existing AWS commitments) [1]. GPT models remain deepest and earliest on Azure OpenAI, but since roughly June 2026 have also reached general availability on Amazon Bedrock [see [Bedrock vs Azure OpenAI](/comparisons/bedrock-vs-azure-openai/)]. An organization with committed AWS spend is no longer choosing between "Claude, which draws down my AWS commitment" and "GPT, which doesn't" — both can now draw down an AWS commitment, though Azure remains the deeper, first-party home for OpenAI's models specifically.

*Fetched directly from Anthropic's and OpenAI's own pricing pages this session [1][8]; both change frequently and both providers reserve the right to change promotional rates without much notice — Anthropic itself cancelled a scheduled Sonnet 5 price increase in August 2026, and OpenAI's Sol pricing above is explicitly labelled promotional with a stated (not permanent) expiry. Model the number that's dated, not the number that's current.*

*One simplification in the table above, worth naming rather than leaving implicit: every GPT-5.6 and GPT-6 tier bills a second, roughly double rate once a request's context crosses OpenAI's long-context threshold — GPT-6 Astra, for instance, is $10/$50 short-context but $20/$75 beyond it, and Sol, Terra, and Luna each step up the same way [8]. Claude's per-token price, by contrast, is flat across its full 1M-token context window regardless of request length [1]. A workload that regularly sends long-context requests to a GPT tier should budget against the higher figure, not the headline short-context one shown here.*

**Prompt caching mechanics now diverge in a way that compounds at scale.** Every other current Claude model uses a 0.1x multiplier on cached-token reads; Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 use 0.025x — a genuine structural change, not just a sticker-price one, and Anthropic states it lowers typical workload cost by roughly 25% and highly agentic (heavy re-read) workload cost by up to 45% [2]. For a workload that is mostly single-pass rather than cache-heavy, this difference barely registers; for a long-running agentic session that re-reads large cached context every turn, it is the single largest cost lever on this table. Batch APIs on both sides offer a roughly 50% discount for non-real-time work.

**The biggest cost lever remains model selection within a provider, not provider selection between them.** Routing simple classification or extraction to Haiku 4.5 or GPT-5.6 Luna instead of a flagship tier can cut cost by an order of magnitude with acceptable quality for many tasks — a lever available regardless of which vendor's flagship you also use.

## Resilience and continuity

**This is where a single, dated, real event should carry real weight in the decision, not just a hypothetical "outages happen" paragraph.** On 12 June 2026, a US national-security order directed Anthropic to cut off foreign access to Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5; because the restriction was not practical to enforce per-user, Anthropic took both models offline for everyone worldwide, with essentially no notice [9]. Access was restored roughly three weeks later, from 1 July 2026, with usage capped during the ramp-back [10]. This is, as far as this wiki's reporting can confirm, the first time the US government restricted access to a specific commercial AI model itself rather than the export of hardware. It happened at the frontier tier specifically (Fable/Mythos), not to Sonnet, Opus, or Haiku, and — per the gating discussion above — the pattern of restricting the most cyber-capable tier is now visibly repeating with GPT-6 Astra's own launch-day safeguards [3][4]. Whether a comparable shutdown could reach a mid-tier model on either side is not something this page, or anyone outside the relevant government offices, can currently answer — name it as unresolved rather than implying either lineup is safe from it.

**Multi-path reach has converged somewhat but is not identical.** Claude is reachable through four largely independent paths: Anthropic's own API, AWS Bedrock, Google Cloud Vertex AI, and Microsoft Foundry. GPT is reachable through three: OpenAI's own API, Azure OpenAI (the deepest, first-party path), and now Amazon Bedrock. Neither is a true single-vendor dependency any more, which narrows what used to be a clean differentiator — but a reader whose resilience requirement is specifically "redundancy across clouds with no shared parent infrastructure" currently has one more independent path available for Claude (Google Cloud) than for GPT. Whether that extra path matters depends on what kind of outage the reader is actually defending against — a single-region failure, a single-cloud failure, or the kind of single-model, government-ordered restriction described above, which no amount of multi-cloud reach would have prevented, since Anthropic disabled the model across every one of its own hosting paths at once.

**A second, smaller data point landed the same week this page was last verified, and it is worth naming precisely because it is a different kind of event from the June shutdown.** On the morning of 3 September 2026, OpenAI and Anthropic each confirmed their own real, status-page-logged incidents that overlapped for roughly eighty minutes — ChatGPT and Codex degraded from 14:58 to 16:55 UTC, Claude (including Opus 5 and Fable 5.1) from 13:26 to 16:16 UTC — with no shared root cause established by either company or by outside reporting, despite speculation pointing at Azure [see [ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini go down the same morning](/news/openai-anthropic-google-outages-sept-2026/)]. This was ordinary operational failure, not a policy action, and both providers recovered within hours rather than weeks, so it does not carry the same weight as the June order. But it is a second, independent, dated reminder that outages are not hypothetical on either side of this comparison, and that a multi-provider failover strategy only helps if it has actually been exercised — a team routing all production traffic to one vendor had no good options during either provider's window that morning.

See [preparing for AI provider restrictions](/guides/preparing-for-ai-provider-restrictions/) for the concrete architectural response — abstracting the provider behind an interface so a restriction, an outage, or a deprecation is a config change rather than a rebuild — which applies regardless of which lineup a reader currently prefers.

## Vendor lock-in, exit cost, and data gravity

**Platform breadth is a real, if narrow, gate for a specific requirement.** OpenAI ships embeddings, image generation, and speech (transcription and TTS) alongside its language models under one account and one contract; Anthropic does not ship any of these directly. A reader whose actual requirement is "one vendor, one contract, five modalities" has that requirement gate out an Anthropic-only stack — not because Claude is worse at any one of those tasks, but because the product simply is not offered. That gate disappears the moment a second vendor for those specific modalities is acceptable, which is the common case; naming it as a gate only for the narrower "single-vendor-only" reader avoids overstating it.

**Fine-tuning support is closer to a gate than a soft tradeoff for a specific requirement, too.** OpenAI documents broad fine-tuning support across its model lineup; Anthropic's own documentation has historically described fine-tuning as limited availability rather than a standard self-serve product. This page could not re-fetch Anthropic's current fine-tuning documentation this session to confirm that has not changed, so treat the direction (OpenAI broader, Anthropic narrower) as the durable pattern and confirm the specifics directly before a roadmap depends on it. A roadmap that specifically depends on deep, self-managed fine-tuning at scale should treat this as a near-gate rather than a minor point in OpenAI's favor.

**API surface differences create real switching cost independent of model quality**, the software-licensing sense of lock-in applied to a proprietary API rather than a proprietary license: OpenAI's Structured Outputs and Responses API versus Anthropic's tool-use-based structured extraction and Messages API are not drop-in replacements for each other, and code written against one requires real rework to move to the other. See [software licensing and vendor lock-in](/guides/software-licensing-and-vendor-lock-in/) for why "can we read the docs" is a different question from "can we leave," and [cloud exit costs and data gravity](/guides/cloud-exit-costs-and-data-gravity/) for the general mechanics of what actually resists moving once it exists.

**One place lock-in is shallower than it looks:** a RAG index or knowledge base built against a specific embedding model does not, by itself, lock you to that provider's *chat* model. Bedrock Knowledge Bases can point at either provider's chat models behind the same retrieval layer regardless of which embeddings produced the index, and OpenAI's own embedding models are available on Bedrock alongside Claude's chat models. An organization that assumes "we already built our RAG pipeline on one vendor's embeddings, so we're locked into their chat model too" should check that assumption before it drives the decision — it is frequently not true.

## Trust and legal control

Distinct from the jurisdictional analysis above, some organizations have a reason — reputational, contractual, or a customer's own requirement — to care about who else has a financial stake in a vendor, independent of whether that stake creates any lawful access right today. Anthropic is independent and privately held, with Amazon and Google as major investors and cloud/compute partners; OpenAI's primary strategic partner and investor is Microsoft [see [OpenAI vs Anthropic](/comparisons/openai-vs-anthropic/)]. An organization that competes directly with Amazon, Google, or Microsoft has a legitimate, if soft, reason to weigh which vendor's cap table includes which competitor — this is not a regulatory question and a feature table would never surface it. This page cannot tell a specific reader whether that relationship matters to their specific competitive situation; that judgment call belongs to the reader's own legal or executive review, not to a general comparison.

## Internal capability

Whichever cloud path a reader already operates is the lower-friction one regardless of model quality: a team fluent in IAM, VPC, CloudTrail, and Bedrock guardrails absorbs Claude-on-AWS with little new operational learning; a team fluent in Entra ID, Azure RBAC, and Azure Monitor absorbs GPT-on-Azure the same way. Moving either model to the *other* team's unfamiliar cloud is a real cost that a benchmark comparison never shows, and it cuts in both directions depending on which cloud the reader's team actually knows. Newer, more distinctive surfaces — OpenAI's Responses API and multi-agent orchestration beta, Anthropic's Managed Agents and tool-use patterns — each require genuinely new operational skill regardless of vendor; neither side is the "no new skills required" option.

## What this comparison cannot resolve

**Whether a specific downstream system is Annex III high-risk under the EU AI Act** depends on facts about that system, not on this page, and not on which model it calls — get that classification from [governance thresholds as you scale](/guides/governance-thresholds-as-you-scale/) and, if needed, a compliance review specific to the use case.

**Whether a specific US state's AI law currently binds a specific deployment** is unsettled at the federal-preemption level and will be decided by litigation and Congress on a timeline this page cannot predict.

**Whether either provider's investor relationships matter to a specific reader's competitive situation** is a judgment call for that organization's own legal or executive review, not a fact this page can settle generally.

**Whether a repeat of the June 2026 Fable/Mythos shutdown could reach a mid-tier model, on either side,** is not knowable from public information as of this writing. The only honest statement available is that it has happened once, at the frontier tier, to one provider, and that the same provider has since demonstrated it can restore service within weeks — not that it cannot happen again, to either lineup.

**The exact dollar cost of a jurisdictional guarantee routed specifically through Azure** — as distinct from OpenAI's own first-party API, which does publish an explicit 10% data-residency uplift comparable to Anthropic's 1.1x — is not published as a single multiplier; a reader deploying through Azure OpenAI or Azure Government specifically should get a direct, current quote rather than assume either vendor's first-party number carries over.
