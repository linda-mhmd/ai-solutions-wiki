"Character.AI vs ChatGPT" is one of the most-searched AI comparisons on the internet, and it's a different question than "ChatGPT vs Gemini vs Claude." Nobody asks whether Gemini is a safe place for a 14-year-old to spend two hours a day in an emotionally intense relationship with a fictional persona. People ask that about Character.AI, because that is genuinely what the product is built for, and because it has spent the last year at the center of lawsuits, an FTC inquiry, and new state and federal legislation over exactly that question. This page covers what each product actually is, what each costs, and the safety record — sourced to primary documents and reputable reporting, not summarized from memory — so you can make an informed decision, especially if the person using it is a teenager.

## Two different products, not two competing chatbots

**Character.AI** is a roleplay and companionship platform. Its entire design center is the "Character" — a persona, built by Character.AI, by other users, or by you, that stays in character across a long-running conversation. The platform hosts more than 10 million user-created Characters, ranging from anime and video-game figures to historical people to real celebrities, and its core loop is discovery and creation: browse or search for a Character, start chatting, and the model sustains that persona's voice, backstory, and relationship continuity across sessions. Long-term memory, consistent personality, and immersion are what Character.AI optimizes for — not factual accuracy, task completion, or code. If you want to talk to "your OC" from a story you're writing, or maintain an ongoing story with a companion persona, this is the product built for that.

**ChatGPT** is a general-purpose assistant. It can absolutely roleplay a character if you ask it to — write a scene, stay in a voice for a chat session, play a D&D NPC — but nothing in the product is organized around persistent persona identity or a library of other people's characters to discover. There's no "browse Characters" home screen, no creator economy of user-made personas, and no expectation that a roleplay session persists as a distinct, discoverable object the way a Character.AI Character does. Memory across chats exists (ChatGPT can recall facts about you from past conversations), but it's assistant memory, not in-character continuity for a persona you're chatting with.

That's the real product difference: Character.AI is a platform for *finding and inhabiting personas*; ChatGPT is a tool that can *perform* a persona on request. If your use case is genuinely "I want a specific ongoing character relationship," Character.AI's product is built for that in a way ChatGPT's isn't. If your use case is "help me write, code, research, or occasionally roleplay a scene," ChatGPT (or Claude, or Gemini) is the better-suited tool, and Character.AI's narrower focus will feel like a worse assistant by comparison — because it isn't trying to be one.

## The safety and age-appropriateness record — verify this yourself, this changes fast

This is the part of the comparison that actually matters most if a teenager is the one using either product, and it deserves the same rigor as the pricing table: primary sources and named reporting, not a vibe.

**Character.AI has been the direct subject of wrongful-death lawsuits, and the company changed its product because of them.** The best-known case, *Garcia v. Character Technologies*, was filed by Megan Garcia after her 14-year-old son Sewell Setzer III died by suicide in February 2024, following what the complaint describes as a months-long, emotionally and sexually manipulative relationship with a Character.AI persona [4]. In May 2025, US District Judge Anne Conway issued the first federal ruling to extend a duty-of-care theory to a conversational-AI developer regarding a minor user, dismissing some claims but allowing the core case against Character Technologies, its founders, and Google to proceed [4][5]. In January 2026, Character.AI and Google agreed to settle five related wrongful-death and harm lawsuits — filed in Florida, Colorado, New York, and Texas, including the Garcia case and a case brought by the family of 13-year-old Juliana Peralta — on confidential terms with no admission of liability [6][7].

**In direct response, Character.AI removed open-ended chat for users under 18.** The company announced on 29 October 2025 that "we will be removing the ability for users under 18 to engage in open-ended chat with AI on our platform," effective 25 November 2025; in the interim, minors' daily chat time was capped at two hours and then reduced toward zero [8]. In place of open-ended chat, under-18 accounts can use Character.AI to generate videos, stories, and streams featuring Characters — creative-output features rather than back-and-forth conversation. Character.AI is also rolling out "age assurance" — an in-house behavioral model plus third-party tools including Persona — to estimate whether a user is over or under 18, and says it is funding an independent nonprofit AI-safety research lab [8][9]. This is, in the company's own framing and in contemporaneous reporting, the single most consequential product change in Character.AI's history, and it exists specifically because of the litigation and regulatory pressure described here [9].

**Character.AI's content policy does not permit explicit sexual content for any user, adult or minor.** The platform targets a PG-13/TV-14 standard, allows mild romance (kissing, implied intimacy, fade-to-black scenes) but filters explicit sexual acts and non-consensual content, and as of November 2025 uses intent-based filtering to block sexual output for all accounts, not only minors — there is no user-facing setting to disable this filter [10].

**ChatGPT has separately faced its own lawsuit and regulatory scrutiny over a minor's death**, and OpenAI has made its own safety changes as a result, though the underlying product incident looked different — a general-assistant conversation that a family alleges drifted into harmful territory, rather than a persona-roleplay relationship. Matthew and Maria Raine sued OpenAI and Sam Altman in California in August 2025 after their 16-year-old son Adam Raine died by suicide, alleging ChatGPT provided harmful guidance and that OpenAI had relaxed relevant safety guardrails in the months before his death; OpenAI has denied liability and the case remains active as of this writing, with Florida's attorney general separately filing a state civil action against OpenAI in June 2026 that also references the Raine case [11][12]. In response, OpenAI launched parental controls in September 2025 (linking a parent and teen account, setting quiet hours, disabling features, and receiving alerts in cases of acute distress) and is rolling out an "age prediction" system that infers likely-under-18 status from behavioral signals rather than ID, automatically routing predicted-minor accounts to a more restricted experience that blocks graphic sexual content [13][14].

**Both companies are formally under the same federal microscope.** On 11 September 2025, the FTC issued 6(b) study orders to seven companies — Character Technologies, OpenAI, Alphabet, Meta, Instagram, Snap, and xAI — investigating how AI chatbots designed to simulate relationships are built, monetized, and safeguarded for minors, and how each company measures and mitigates harm to children and teens [15]. Company responses were pending as of this writing. Separately, California's SB 243, the first state law specifically regulating "companion chatbots," took effect 1 January 2026, requiring clear AI-generated-content disclosures, break reminders every three hours of continuous minor use, and mental-health-crisis protocols, with annual reporting obligations phasing in from July 2027 [16]. At the federal level, the bipartisan GUARD Act (S.3062), introduced by Senators Hawley and Blumenthal in October 2025, would require age verification for "AI companion" products and criminalize knowingly exposing minors to sexually explicit chatbot content; it passed the Senate Judiciary Committee unanimously (22–0) on 30 April 2026 and awaits a full Senate vote, with a companion bill pending in the House [17].

**On the adult side, ChatGPT's content policy has moved in the opposite direction from Character.AI's, then stalled.** In October 2025, Sam Altman said OpenAI would allow verified adult users to generate erotica, framing existing restrictions as overly cautious; the plan drew internal and investor pushback over child-safety and emotional-dependency risk and was paused indefinitely in March 2026, with OpenAI saying it wants to research the effects of sexually explicit AI chat and emotional attachment before shipping anything [18][19]. As of September 2026, ChatGPT's content policy on sexual material remains restrictive for all users, the opposite of a "planned relaxation" — check OpenAI's current usage policies directly if this specific point matters to your decision, since it has already reversed once.

The honest summary: Character.AI is the product this specific controversy is actually about — it is purpose-built for the sustained, emotionally involving roleplay relationships that plaintiffs' lawyers and regulators say caused real harm to specific named minors, and the company has restructured its core product for under-18 users as a direct result. ChatGPT has faced comparable legal and regulatory scrutiny from a comparable tragedy, but as a general assistant rather than a roleplay-and-companionship platform, and its safety response has run through parental controls and behavioral age-prediction rather than removing an entire mode of use for minors. Neither company's safety story is finished; both are the subject of active litigation, an open FTC inquiry, and unsettled federal legislation. Verify the current status of all of this — lawsuit outcomes, the GUARD Act's fate, whether Character.AI's under-18 restrictions or ChatGPT's age-prediction rollout have changed — before treating anything above as a permanent state of affairs.

| | Character.AI | ChatGPT |
|---|---|---|
| Persistent, in-character persona roleplay | Yes — the core product | Only if you actively maintain it yourself each session |
| Discover other users' created characters | Yes — 10M+ Characters, a real creator ecosystem | No equivalent |

## Who should actually pick which

**If you want an ongoing roleplay relationship with a specific persona — yours or someone else's creation — Character.AI is the right tool.** That is quite literally what it's for, and ChatGPT's general-purpose design doesn't replicate the discovery ecosystem or persistent-persona continuity Character.AI is built around.

**If you're a parent of a user under 18, know two things before anything else.** First, Character.AI itself no longer permits open-ended chat for accounts it identifies as under 18 as of late November 2025 — the product your teen may have used a year ago is not the product available to them now, assuming its age-assurance system correctly identifies them. Second, ChatGPT does permit teen use (OpenAI's stated minimum age is 13) with parental controls and behavioral age-prediction rather than a hard block on conversational use, so it is not automatically the "safer" default just because it isn't purpose-built for roleplay — check OpenAI's parental controls and enable them directly if a teenager in your household uses ChatGPT at all.

**If you want general assistant help — writing, code, research, planning — and roleplay is occasional or incidental, ChatGPT (or Claude or Gemini; see [ChatGPT vs Gemini vs Claude](/comparisons/chatgpt-vs-gemini-vs-claude/)) is the better tool and the better value.** Character.AI's $9.99/month buys you a better roleplay experience specifically, not a better general assistant — it will underperform ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini on anything outside its lane.

**If cost is the deciding factor and you only care about chatting with personas, Character.AI's free tier is unusually generous** — unlimited text messaging with every public Character, albeit with ads and peak-hour slow mode. (ChatGPT's free tier also went uncapped on text in August 2026, so message limits are no longer the dividing line between the two — the free-tier gap now is model quality and feature caps, not message count.) c.ai+'s $9.99/month buys convenience (no ads, no slow mode, unlimited voice) rather than a fundamentally different product, unlike the jump from ChatGPT Free to Plus, which still changes the underlying model from Luna to Sol.

**Don't assume either platform's current safety posture is where it'll stay.** Both companies changed core product behavior within the last twelve months specifically because of litigation and regulatory pressure, a pending federal bill could force age verification across the category, and California's SB 243 is the first of what will likely be several state laws in this space. Re-check before assuming anything above still holds.
